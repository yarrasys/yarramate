# Compile scale — incremental entry point measurements (2026-08-17)

Status: final. Every number below was produced by a harness in this directory
on the machine described under Versions and provenance, and every harness is
runnable by a reader. Numbers that changed after a measurement defect was found
are reported here with the defect, not silently replaced.

These measurements answer a consumer brief: a per-commit consumer compiles a
project's whole architecture model on every write inside a Cloudflare Durable
Object, and asked for a delta entry point. The design decision they informed is
[ADR 0091](../../adr/0091-reuse-is-decided-by-source-text-not-a-declared-delta.md).

## Versions and provenance

- Repository at `1ba85e6`, `package.json` version `0.19.0`; both measured
  changes (`compileWorkspaceIncremental`, single-parse + position memo) are
  unreleased and land in `0.20.0`.
- Toolchain: Node v26.3.0, macOS 26.6.1, Apple M5 Pro, 64 GiB RAM. Every run
  against `dist/` built by `pnpm build` at that commit.
- Harnesses (this directory, all invoked as `node <harness> <sizes...>`):
  - `measure.mjs` — cold whole-workspace compile, canonical serialization, and
    digest, measured separately, plus peak RSS.
  - `delta.mjs` — what a per-commit consumer pays: the same final workspace
    compiled from scratch versus compiled with the previous commit's cache.
  - `memory.mjs` — retained versus transient heap attribution at one size.
  - `cache-memory.mjs` — what holding the returned cache costs.
  - `cap.mjs` — does one commit fit a Durable Object, cold and warm, under a
    448 MB heap (`--max-old-space-size=448`).
- Corpus: synthetic, generated by `generateWorkspace` in `measure.mjs`. One
  concept and three cross-document relationships per document, so
  cross-document reference resolution is included at a 1:3 concept:relationship
  mix. Claim values are realistic rather than empty.
- Each size runs in its own process, so peak RSS is attributable and no run
  inherits another's heap.

## Cold compile — the baseline the delta is measured against

`measure.mjs`, one process per size:

| documents | claims | compile ms | µs/doc | serialize ms | digest ms | peak RSS MB |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1,000 | 9,000 | 192.8 | 193 | 7.7 | 2.5 | 194 |
| 10,000 | 90,000 | 1,431.2 | 143 | 80.5 | 24.9 | 618 |
| 40,000 | 360,000 | 5,285.2 | 132 | 347.9 | 114.0 | 2,023 |

Compile is linear in documents. It is also 33% faster than it was before this
work: `compileWorkspaceResolved` parsed every source twice (once to read its
`format` key for profile-versus-document classification, then again in its own
loop), and re-parsed a document to resolve each claim's line and column. At
40,000 documents the two-parse path measured 7,939 ms; reading the composed
value the first parse produced and memoising positions per document measures
5,285 ms.

## The delta — what a per-commit consumer pays

`delta.mjs`, one process per size, `--expose-gc`. A commit appends 40 documents
to a workspace of the stated size. `full ms` compiles the same final sources
from scratch; `delta ms` compiles them with the previous commit's cache;
`proportional ms` is what the full compile scaled by the changed fraction would
cost, i.e. the cost of a hypothetically change-proportional compile.

| documents | appended | full ms | delta ms | delta share | proportional ms |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1,000 | 40 | 172.7 | 21.7 | 0.126 | 6.6 |
| 5,000 | 40 | 694.5 | 79.9 | 0.115 | 5.5 |
| 10,000 | 40 | 1,330.7 | 190.1 | 0.143 | 5.3 |
| 20,000 | 40 | 2,592.3 | 354.0 | 0.137 | 5.2 |
| 40,000 | 40 | 5,431.1 | 697.5 | 0.128 | 5.4 |

**A delta is ~13% of a full compile, and is not proportional to the change.**
Byte-identity of the two paths is asserted untimed inside the harness at every
size: if the delta's serialized graph and profile-context differ from the full
compile's, the harness throws rather than reporting a timing.

The residual is not parse. Profiling only the delta calls (`node:inspector`
session started after the seed compile, six delta calls at 40,000 documents):

| bucket | share of samples |
|---|---:|
| claim emission (`compileWorkspaceResolved`, closures, kind resolution) | 50% |
| total-order sort (`compareById`) | 26% |
| garbage collection | 12% |
| YAML parse (the 40 appended documents only) | 0.5% |

The cache does what it claims — 0.5% parse means only changed documents are
parsed — and what remains is whole-workspace work: claim emission for every
document, one total order over all claims and subjects, and the cross-document
validation passes (duplicate ids, unresolved references, endpoint coherence,
whole-part contradiction). Claim emission is not per-document pure: it
interleaves the cross-document lookups that produce diagnostics, so caching a
document's claims would not let that document be skipped.

### The control: the same cache without the position memo

The parse cache alone does not produce the delta. Patching `positionReader` in a
scratch worktree at the same commit so it resolves positions by re-parsing
instead of reading the memo, and changing nothing else:

| documents | appended | full ms | delta ms | delta share |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1,000 | 40 | 159.8 | 138.1 | 0.864 |
| 40,000 | 40 | 5,348.2 | 5,078.4 | 0.950 |

Every emitted claim carries a `source` line and column, so a cached document is
re-parsed the first time one of its claims asks for a position — the cache hit
saves the composition and pays the parse anyway. The memo is what turns 95%
into 13%.

## Memory — the ceiling this work does not move

`memory.mjs`, one process per size, `--expose-gc`. Peak is measured after
compile with the parse scratch still reachable; retained is what stays when
only the compiled result is held; transient is the difference a delta skips.

| documents | claims | sources MB | peak MB | retained MB | transient MB | bytes/claim |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1,000 | 9,000 | 1 | 31 | 5 | 18 | 583 |
| 10,000 | 90,000 | 11 | 218 | 47 | 163 | 548 |
| 40,000 | 360,000 | 47 | 767 | 184 | 575 | 536 |

Holding the returned cache (`cache-memory.mjs`) costs less than the result it
accompanies:

| documents | claims | result + cache MB | result only MB | cache MB |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1,000 | 9,000 | 7 | 5 | 2 |
| 10,000 | 90,000 | 66 | 41 | 25 |
| 40,000 | 360,000 | 263 | 137 | 126 |

`cap.mjs` under a 448 MB heap — a 512 MiB Durable Object with headroom for the
runtime — bisects the ceiling. `cold` compiles with no cache (what a consumer
pays on the first request after an eviction); `warm` compiles with the previous
commit's cache resident:

| documents | mode | outcome | peak RSS MB | heap used MB |
|---:|---|---|---:|---:|
| 15,000 | cold | compiled | 448 | 315 |
| 15,000 | warm | compiled | 505 | 215 |
| 20,000 | cold | compiled | 545 | 405 |
| 20,000 | warm | compiled | 528 | 267 |
| 25,000 | cold | OOM | — | — |
| 25,000 | warm | OOM | — | — |

**The ceiling is 20,000 documents, and holding the cache lowers heap rather
than raising it** (267 MB warm vs 405 MB cold at 20,000) because the cache
replaces the parse scratch a cold compile has to rebuild. The consumer's stated
target of 40,000 documents does not fit a 512 MiB Durable Object at any speed:
the compiled graph alone is 184 MB retained, 767 MB peak. The CPU blocker the
brief reported (28 minutes of accumulated compile per project, DO reset for
exceeding its CPU limit) is cleared with a 43× margin against a 30 s budget;
the next ceiling is memory, not compile.

## Rejected alternative, measured

`Intl.Collator` as a replacement for `localeCompare` in the total-order sort:
same order (byte-identical output), 2× slower — 207 ms versus 97 ms over
360,000 synthetic ids. V8 already fast-paths `localeCompare` for this shape.

## Threats to validity

- **Single machine, single OS, no repetition statistics.** Every table is one
  run per cell on one Apple M5 Pro. Ratios (delta share, parse share, warm vs
  cold heap) are the load-bearing claims and are stable across the sizes;
  absolute milliseconds are not portable to a Cloudflare worker's CPU.
- **Synthetic corpus.** `generateWorkspace` emits a regular 1:3
  concept:relationship shape with uniform claim sizes. A real workspace has
  irregular document sizes and reference locality, which would change constant
  factors and could change the sort's share.
- **The delta is measured for appends.** `delta.mjs` appends 40 documents. The
  property test (`test/compile-incremental.test.ts`, 24 seeded sequences × 12
  steps) exercises edits and deletions for *identity*, but the timing tables
  here do not separate append from edit from delete.
- **A measurement defect was found and corrected in this pass.** The delta
  harness originally timed the full compile while the seed compile's graph was
  still reachable, so the full-compile figure carried collection cost the delta
  path never paid. That inflated the delta's advantage: the first draft of ADR
  0091 published a 7.2% delta share at 40,000 documents, and the corrected
  harness (seed released, both paths timed under equal heap, `--expose-gc`
  between them) measures 12.8%. The corrected figure is the one published above
  and in the ADR.
- **A second defect, in the fix itself.** The position memo initially dropped
  the parsed document at the end of `parseWorkspaceSource`, so a cold compile
  re-parsed each source on its first position read — reintroducing the double
  parse it was meant to remove and measuring 9,438 ms at 40,000 documents. A
  source parsed in the current call now hands its composed document straight to
  the position reader. The 5,285 ms above is the fixed path.
- **Profiler overhead is inside the residual profile.** The `node:inspector`
  session's own `post` frames account for ~7% of samples in the delta profile;
  bucket shares above are of total samples including that overhead, so the
  compiler-side shares are mild underestimates.
- **Peak RSS is not heap.** The `cap.mjs` OOM boundary is a V8 old-space limit
  (`--max-old-space-size=448`), which is the closest local analogue to a
  Durable Object's memory cap but not the same accounting a worker applies.
